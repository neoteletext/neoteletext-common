# The synthesizers

NeoTeletext's audio profile plays instruments as well as recorded sound. A channel sends what a player would do (notes, patches, register writes), and the viewer's browser plays it on an instrument built into the browser, at 48,000 samples a second. No recording is sent.

Each instrument is NeoTeletext's own, written from public documents: service manuals, schematics, datasheets, patents and published measurements. Each models the behaviour of a well-known instrument of the 1970s and 1980s, and has a NeoTeletext name of its own.

## The instruments

| NeoTeletext name | What it is | The instrument it models |
|---|---|---|
| **synth6581** | A three-voice sound chip with one filter | The MOS Technology 6581, the sound chip of the Commodore 64 |
| **synth8580** | The same chip's later revision, with a different filter and combined waveforms | The MOS Technology 8580 |
| **synth7** | A six-operator FM synthesizer, sixteen notes over sixteen parts | The Yamaha DX7 |
| **synth106** | A six-voice analogue synthesizer with a digitally clocked oscillator and a chorus | The Roland Juno-106 |
| **synth60** | Its predecessor, with its own filter positions and chorus settings | The Roland Juno-60 |
| **synthD** | A monophonic analogue synthesizer with three oscillators and a ladder filter | The Moog Minimoog Model D |
| **synth53** | A five-voice analogue synthesizer | The Sequential Circuits Prophet-5, revision 3 |
| **synth52** | The same instrument with the earlier oscillator and filter chips | The Sequential Circuits Prophet-5, revision 2 |
| **synth51** | As synth52 | The Sequential Circuits Prophet-5, revision 1 |
| **synth8** | An eight-voice analogue synthesizer with two oscillators a voice | The Roland Jupiter-8 |
| **synth80** | An eight-voice analogue synthesizer with two channels a voice and touch response | The Yamaha CS-80 |

synth51 and synth52 are one model today: revisions 1 and 2 of the instrument they model share their oscillator and filter chips, and nothing yet tells them apart.
